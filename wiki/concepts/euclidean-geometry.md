# Euclidean Geometry

The geometry developed from Euclid's definitions, postulates, common notions, and propositions. It describes the familiar geometry of points, lines, planes, angles, circles, triangles, polygons, and solids under Euclid's assumptions.

## Notes

Euclidean geometry is important both for its content and for its structure. It models how a knowledge system can derive many reliable results from a small set of explicit starting assumptions.
