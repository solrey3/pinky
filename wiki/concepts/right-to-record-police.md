# Right to Record Police

The right to record police is the First Amendment protection for observing and documenting law-enforcement activity conducted in public, subject to reasonable restrictions that prevent actual interference with police work. It supports public oversight by allowing citizens and journalists to create independent evidence of government conduct.

## Notes

Recognition of the right does not eliminate disputes over distance, obstruction, scene safety, or qualified immunity. The central distinction is between recording public activity and materially interfering with an officer's duties.
