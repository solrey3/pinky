## Synthesis Notes

Starlink and Flock Safety illustrate a shared governance problem: privately operated technical systems can become consequential public infrastructure. One controls communications performance in a war zone; the other turns routine vehicle movement into searchable law-enforcement records. In both cases, legitimacy depends on transparent authority, scoped control, auditability, and enforceable remedies rather than vendor discretion alone.

The refrigeration report extends critical-infrastructure cybersecurity into retail food systems, where insecure connected equipment can affect fire safety, inventory, and business continuity. The Philippine items connect consumer inflation, alleged market coordination, and climate-related supply preparation, but the dispatch does not establish causal relationships and the claims require regulator and agency confirmation.
