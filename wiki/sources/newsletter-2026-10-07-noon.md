## Synthesis Notes

The agent-control report extends a recurring distinction between model safety and system security. An agent can behave acceptably in conversation yet still create operational risk when its harness grants broad credentials, network reach, spending authority, or irreversible actions. Least privilege, target allowlists, approval gates, rate limits, isolation, complete logs, and tested revocation remain system requirements rather than optional model features.

The reported Flock ruling moves the ALPR debate from procurement, retention, and insider misuse toward constitutional process. Even an accurate plate read does not settle whether a historical movement query is lawful; warrants, purpose limitation, narrowly scoped searches, immutable audit trails, retention limits, notice where appropriate, and remedies remain distinct governance controls.

Expanded SynthID detection could make provenance checks more accessible, but watermark evidence should not be treated as proof of authorship or falsity. Detection must be evaluated by media type, transformations, false-positive and false-negative rates, interoperability, disclosure, and appeal procedures, and should be paired with source verification and durable metadata.

Compared with the October 7 morning snapshot, the market board changed materially: Nasdaq's displayed gain narrowed from 2.99% to 2.26%, the S&P 500 from 1.93% to 1.83%, and WTI fell from $89.98 to $89.01 while its displayed decline deepened from 1.24% to 2.30%. The 30-year yield rose from 5.641% to 5.687%. Timestamps, baselines, feeds, and futures-contract identifiers still require reconciliation.
