## Summary

The September 18 evening brief pairs institutional transition with disputes over market and information access. Warren Buffett reportedly plans to leave the Berkshire Hathaway chair, marking a major succession point for a company closely associated with his capital-allocation approach. The SEC reportedly moved tokenized stocks and round-the-clock trading closer to implementation, while the Trump administration's reported exclusion of CNN, MS NOW, and Politico from the White House raised broader press-access and First Amendment concerns. A new U.S.–Greenland–Denmark security agreement, litigation over Montana's anti-deepfake law, and planned FAA spending on an AI air-traffic tool rounded out the policy and technology agenda.

Markets remained sharply divided. Nasdaq gained 0.72% and AMD rose 8.46%, but the Dow lost 1.69%, the Russell 2000 fell 1.50%, every tracked ETF declined, and Netflix dropped 7.25%. WTI fell 5.84% to $95.47, easing immediate energy pressure while remaining historically elevated; Bitcoin gained 3.96%. Long borrowing costs stayed restrictive, with the 30-year Treasury at 5.331% and a quoted 30-year mortgage rate of 7.09%.

Sports coverage included Pelle Larsson's reported extension, Aaron Judge's calf injury, football and baseball results, European soccer, and final Tour de France standings. The wrestling section tracked upcoming WWE, AEW, ROH, TNA, NJPW, and CMLL programming and recent highly rated matches.

## Synthesis Notes

The tokenized-stock item corroborates the morning and noon reports and shifts the topic from speculative infrastructure toward an active market-structure question. Extended hours and programmable settlement may reduce friction, but they also increase demands on custody, surveillance, liquidity provision, operational resilience, and investor protection.

The White House access restrictions extend the wiki's press-freedom thread from prior restraint and regulatory pressure to selective access controlled by the executive branch. The constitutional and practical questions are related but distinct: officials may manage limited space and credentials, yet viewpoint-based exclusion can impair newsgathering and public accountability.

The close preserves the recurring narrow-growth pattern: semiconductor and Nasdaq strength coexist with steep Dow and small-cap losses, broad ETF weakness, and high long rates. WTI's decline from the noon quote of $97.12 to $95.47 is directionally consistent, though the adjacent dispatches' rapid sequence should still be reconciled by timestamp and contract.
