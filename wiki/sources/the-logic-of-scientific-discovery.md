# The Logic of Scientific Discovery — Wisdom Extracted

**Author:** [[Karl Popper]]  
**Date:** 1934 / 1959  
**Source:** [raw/books/The_Logic_of_Scientific_Discovery.md](../../raw/books/The_Logic_of_Scientific_Discovery.md)

## Summary

Karl Popper argues that science is not built by inductively proving theories from repeated observations. Universal theories can never be finally verified by finite evidence, but they can expose themselves to possible refutation. The mark of a scientific theory is therefore [[Falsifiability]]: it must forbid possible observations and risk being wrong.

The book replaces the search for certainty with a fallibilist method of conjecture, severe testing, criticism, and provisional acceptance. Corroboration means a theory has survived meaningful attempts at refutation; it does not mean the theory has been proven true.

## Key Claims

1. **Induction cannot justify universal laws:** No finite sequence of confirming observations logically proves a universal scientific theory.
2. **Falsifiability demarcates science:** A scientific theory must make claims that could conflict with possible observations.
3. **Corroboration is not proof:** Surviving severe tests increases confidence but never converts a theory into certainty.
4. **Ad hoc rescue weakens science:** Modifications that merely protect a theory from refutation reduce empirical content unless they create new testable predictions.
5. **Scientific rationality is critical:** Progress comes from bold conjectures exposed to public criticism and potential defeat.

## Synthesis Notes

Popper gives Pinky a core epistemology for evaluating claims: ask what would make a belief fail. This complements [[Bayesian Thinking]] and [The Signal and the Noise](the-signal-and-the-noise.md) by adding a criticism-first discipline to probabilistic updating. Bayesian reasoning asks how strongly evidence should move a belief; Popper asks whether the belief was exposed to a severe test in the first place.

The book also sharpens the difference between flexible explanation and real knowledge. Like [The Signal and the Noise](the-signal-and-the-noise.md), it warns against mistaking narrative fit for truth; like [Moneyball](moneyball.md), it values methods that survive contact with evidence rather than tradition or authority. Its central habit is portable: define failure conditions before accepting success.
